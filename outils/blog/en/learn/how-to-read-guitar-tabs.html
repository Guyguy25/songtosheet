How to read guitar tabs

The 6 lines, the numbers, chords, symbols and rhythm: everything you need to read a tab and play your first song, even if you can’t read music.

What is a tab?

Tablature (or “tab”) is a way of writing music designed for string instruments. Instead of telling you which note to play, like sheet music, it tells you where to put your fingers: which string and which fret.

That’s why self-taught guitarists love it: you don’t need to read music to use it. In a few minutes, you can play a riff that would have taken hours to work out by ear.

6 lines = 6 strings

A guitar tab has 6 horizontal lines, one per string. The classic trap: they’re in the opposite order to what you see when you look down at your guitar.

  • The top line = the thinnest string, high E (e).
  • The bottom line = the thickest string, low E (E).

Think of pitch: high notes at the top, low notes at the bottom. In standard tuning, from top to bottom:

e|-----------
B|-----------
G|-----------
D|-----------
A|-----------
E|-----------

Numbers = frets

Each number on a line tells you which fret to press on that string:

  • 0: open string, played without pressing any fret.
  • 3: press the 3rd fret.
  • 12: the 12th fret (one number, not 1 then 2).
  • x: muted note, a percussive sound with no pitch.

Example: play the open A string, then the 2nd fret, then the 3rd.

e|-----------
B|-----------
G|-----------
D|-----------
A|--0--2--3--
E|-----------

Reading left to right: single notes and chords

A tab reads left to right, like text. Two rules are enough:

  • Staggered numbers are played one after another (a melody, a riff, an arpeggio).
  • Numbers stacked vertically are played together: that’s a chord.

Here is a C major chord, first strummed, then played note by note as an arpeggio:

e|--0-----------0--
B|--1---------1----
G|--0-------0------
D|--2-----2--------
A|--3---3----------
E|--x--------------

The x on the low E string means you don’t play it, or you mute it.

Symbols to know

Tabs add letters and signs for playing techniques. The most common:

  • h (hammer-on): 5h7, play fret 5 then “hammer” fret 7 with another finger, without picking again.
  • p (pull-off): 7p5, the reverse: pull your finger off fret 7 to sound fret 5.
  • / and \ (slide): 5/7, slide your finger from fret 5 to 7 without lifting it.
  • b (bend): 7b9, push the string to raise fret 7’s pitch up to fret 9’s.
  • ~ (vibrato): make the note waver.
  • PM (palm mute): rest the edge of your hand on the strings near the bridge for a muted sound.
  • <12>: natural harmonic, lightly touch the string above the 12th fret without pressing.

Don’t worry: you don’t need them to start. Learn them when you meet them in a song.

What about rhythm?

That’s the big limit of tabs: spacing doesn’t tell you exactly how long to hold a note. Hand-written tabs online rarely show precise rhythm.

  • Listen to the song while following the tab, to get the rhythm by ear.
  • Use a tab with rhythm (Guitar Pro or MusicXML), which also shows durations.
  • Use a tab synced with the audio: on Tablurio, the generated tab plays along with the song, and you can slow it down.

Tunings and capo

Most tabs are written in standard tuning (E A D G B E). If a song uses another tuning, it’s shown at the top of the tab, for example “Drop D” (low E tuned down to D).

If the tab mentions a capo (for example “capo 2”), the numbers are written relative to the capo: a 0 means the capo fret, not the open string.

Beginner mistakes

  • Flipping the strings: the top line is the thinnest string, not the thickest.
  • Reading “12” as 1 then 2: a two-digit number is a single fret.
  • Playing too fast: start slowly with the right position, then speed up.
  • Not listening: the tab tells you where to play, the song tells you how.

FAQ

Do I need to read music to read tabs?

No. A tab shows the string and fret to play, which is exactly what makes it accessible without music theory. Rhythm is mostly learned by ear.

Which line is the low E string in a tab?

The bottom line. The top line is the thinnest string, high E.

What does 0 mean in a tab?

The string is played open, without pressing any fret.
