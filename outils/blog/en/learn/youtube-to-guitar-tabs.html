How to turn a YouTube song into guitar tabs

Found the song on YouTube but not the tab? Three ways to get from the video to a guitar tab, from the slowest to the fastest.

The problem: the video exists, the tab doesn’t

YouTube has almost everything: studio versions, live shows, acoustic covers, songs you won’t find anywhere else. Tabs, on the other hand, often only exist for the best-known songs, and their quality varies a lot.

There are three ways to go from a YouTube video to a guitar tab. They don’t take the same time or the same skill.

Method 1: search for an existing tab

Type the song title followed by “tab” or “chords” into a search engine. For a well-known song, you’ll often find a tab written by another guitarist.

  • Pros: free and instant.
  • Cons: many songs have no tab, live versions and covers even less, and hand-written tabs often contain mistakes or leave out the rhythm.

Method 2: work it out by ear

This is what experienced musicians do: listen, find each note on the fretboard, then write it down.

  • On YouTube, click ⚙️ then Playback speed and choose 0.75 or 0.5 to hear every note.
  • Find the key and the chords first, then the melody or riff, phrase by phrase.
  • Write it down as you go on a blank tab grid, or in a tab maker.

Pro: it’s great ear training. Con: expect several hours for a whole song as a beginner.

Method 3: generate the tab with AI

Tools like Tablurio listen to the video’s audio, detect the notes and rhythm, then write them as a tab. In practice:

  1. Copy the link of the YouTube video (a regular video or a Short).
  2. Paste it into the guitar tab generator.
  3. Pick guitar, then tab (or standard notation if you read music), and the part of the song to transcribe.
  4. Play along with the tab while the song plays, and slow it down if needed.

It usually takes thirty seconds to a minute. With a paid plan, you can then export the tab to PDF, MIDI or MusicXML.

Which videos give the best tabs?

  • Clean sound: a studio version or a good live recording rather than a phone recording.
  • A clearly audible guitar: solo acoustic guitar, a riff upfront, a solo intro.
  • 10 minutes max: for a longer song, pick the passage you want to practise.

On very busy arrangements (several distorted guitars, an orchestra), some passages will need a touch-up. It’s an automatic transcription: always check the result by ear.

Which method should you pick?

  • A very famous song: start by searching for an existing tab.
  • You want to train your ear: work it out yourself, with the video slowed down.
  • A rare song, a cover, a live version, or no time: generate the tab, then fix the few passages that need it.

FAQ

Can any YouTube video be turned into a tab?

Almost: videos and Shorts up to 10 minutes are accepted. Results are better when the guitar is clearly audible in the mix.

Is it free?

Tablurio gives you 3 free 30-second previews. Full songs and exports come with the Starter, Pro and Lifetime plans.

Is the generated tab accurate?

It gets the vast majority of notes right, but it’s an automatic transcription: a very busy passage may need a touch-up.

Can I open the tab in Guitar Pro?

Yes, through the MusicXML export, which Guitar Pro and MuseScore can open.
